## Part 1 — The "bar raiser" / leadership round

Usually the last round. The interviewer is senior, not necessarily on your
team. They're evaluating whether you'd raise the bar in the company.

**What they're looking for:**
- Judgment under uncertainty
- Self-awareness ("here's a time I was wrong")
- Engineering culture fit (without being a yes-person)
- Curiosity / how you stay current
- Mentorship signals (have you grown others?)

**Common questions:**

1. "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager. What did you do?"
2. "Describe a project where you failed. What did you learn?"
3. "How do you stay current with K8s / cloud tech?"
4. "What would you build differently if you started over?"
5. "What's a strong opinion you hold weakly?"
6. "Why are you choosing engineering for the next 5 years?"

**Answer framework (STAR-ish):**
- **Context** — set the scene briefly (10-20s)
- **Action** — what YOU did (not "the team did") (30-60s)
- **Result** — quantified if possible (10-20s)
- **Reflection** — what you'd do differently (10-20s)

Reflection is the differentiator. Most candidates skip it.

### Have these stories ready:

| Story | Use for |
|---|---|
| The endpointSelector incident | "Time you broke production / unforeseen consequence" |
| The CCNP layered model decision | "Difficult technical decision with trade-offs" |
| The operator project | "Time you took initiative / shipped without being asked" |
| The dashboard migration | "Time you owned an unglamorous but important problem" |
| The fxview comparison work | "Time you had to influence without authority" |

Polish 2-3 of these to ~90 seconds each. The interviewer will dig.

## Part 2 — Negotiation

If an offer comes this week (or strongly imminent):

### 2.1 Don't take the first offer immediately

Standard play:
> "Thank you so much — I'm really excited about <Company>. Can I have
> 48 hours to review and discuss with my family?"

This buys time. Use it to:
- Check other processes you have in flight
- Send "competing offer in hand" emails to laggard companies
- Look at the offer numbers vs market

### 2.2 Counter

Almost never accept the first number. They expect you to push back.

Counter template:
> "I'm really excited about the role and team. The offer is competitive,
> but slightly below what I was expecting given my Cilium / EKS background
> and the open-source work I've shipped. Could we look at adjusting the
> [base / ESOP / joining bonus] by [10-20%]? I'd be ready to sign at <X>
> total."

NEVER:
- Lie about other offers ("I have an offer for 80 LPA" when you don't —
  they will ask for proof)
- Be aggressive or rude
- Counter without anchoring a number

### 2.3 Levers beyond base

If base is fixed:
- ESOP / RSU bump
- Joining bonus (one-time, often easier to grant)
- Sign-on retention (deferred bonus paid at 6/12 months)
- Faster vesting cliff (6 months instead of 12)
- Performance bonus targets
- WFH days
- Start date flex
- Role title (Senior vs Staff Platform Engineer — affects future career)

Pick 2 levers max. Don't haggle on 6 things.

### 2.4 If they say no to your counter

Pause. Don't immediately fold. Say:
> "I appreciate that. Let me think this through over the next 24 hours."

Then reach out to your other active processes:
> "Hi — I'm in late stages with another company. I'd love to give your
> process a fair shot. Is there a way to accelerate to a decision this
> week?"

Sometimes this triggers a faster offer; sometimes it doesn't. Either way
you've learned something.

### 2.5 When to accept

Accept when:
- The number meets your target
- The role/team excites you (re-read the JD; remember why you applied)
- The negotiation has stalled and the marginal gain isn't worth losing the deal

DON'T accept (yet):
- If you have another offer pending in <5 days
- If the number is below your floor
- If you have unresolved questions about the team/role

## Part 3 — Topping up applications

Even if final rounds look good, keep applying. Reasons:
- An offer can fall through (rare but happens — visa issue, sudden hiring freeze, reorg)
- Multiple offers = much better negotiation
- If this whole round fails, you've got Month 5 fallback options

Aim for 5-10 more this week. Reuse the cover letter template + refresh
your tracker.

---

## Part 4 — Take care of yourself

Final rounds are draining. By Week 16 you'll have done 10-15 hours of
live interviewing across 4 weeks. Burnout signs:
- Sleeping <6 hours
- Skipping meals
- Brain feels foggy in interviews
- Stops practicing because you're tired

Mitigation:
- Don't schedule 2 interviews in one day if avoidable
- Walk for 30 min after every interview (resets cortisol)
- Cut LeetCode practice this week if necessary
- One day off this week, mandatory

You'll perform better rested than over-prepared.
